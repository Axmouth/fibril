Every queue partition is a Keratin log, and a log is a series of segment files.
A durable publish is confirmed once an fsync covers its record, so the cost and
timing of those syncs set the latency floor for durable queues. This page
animates how a segment is written, synced, rolled over and reused, and the
changes made in October 2026 that cut the per-sync cost from 4.9 ms to about
0.7 ms and the rollover stall from about 18 ms to about 1 ms.

Each slab below is one segment file. The lighter shade is data written to the
file, the darker shade is data an fsync has made durable. Measured figures come
from three brokers sharing one consumer NVMe drive with durable 1 KiB messages,
and the fsync times in the bubbles are illustrative values at that scale. The
last scene is a proposed design from [resource limits](/development/resource-limits-design/#segment-pool).

## Where the numbers come from

The measurements, commits and conditions behind each scene are rows in the
[optimization log](/development/optimization-log/). The
[tuning ledger](/development/tuning-ledger/) puts these changes beside the ones
that did not pay off, with the trade-offs of each.
